# Meta-Strategy: from "smart enough" to "just executing"

The goal state: I know what I'm building, for whom, why they'll pay, and how
they'll find it — so the remaining work is pure execution. See [[the-goal]].

The trap to avoid: I'm an engineer, so my default failure mode is **building
first**. The strategy below forces selection and demand-proof *before* code.
Code is the cheapest part for me; that's exactly why it's not the bottleneck.

## The pipeline (each stage has an exit criterion — don't move on without it)

### 0. Fix the constraints (one evening)
Decide and write down: hours/week I'll invest (realistic, with a job), max $
per experiment, and a **kill deadline** per idea (e.g., 4 weeks to first
signal or it dies). Exit: numbers written in this folder.

### 1. Diverge — build the idea pool (1–2 weeks, background task)
Generate 30–50 ideas that fit my skills and the no-services rule. Sources:
problems I hit at work, a.team gig patterns (what do people keep paying for?),
Stack Overflow questions I answer, communities I'm already in. Quantity over
quality; no judging while generating. Exit: an `ideas/` list with 30+ entries.

### 2. Converge — score and cut (one sitting)
Score each 1–5 on: **(a)** I can reach the buyers (do I know where they hang
out?), **(b)** buildable MVP in ≤4 weekends, **(c)** passive once running,
**(d)** people already pay for something like it (competition = demand proof,
not a deterrent), **(e)** I'd enjoy tending it for years. Keep top 3.
Exit: 3 finalists with one-line "who pays and why" each.

### 3. Validate demand before building (2–4 weeks, in parallel for all 3)
For each finalist, the cheapest possible test: a landing page with a price and
a waitlist/pre-order button, a post in the community where buyers live, or DM
10 potential buyers. Looking for **pull**: signups, replies, "when can I have
this". Exit: one idea with real signal; kill the rest without mercy.
No signal on any → back to stage 2 with the next 3. This loop is the machine.

### 4. Distribution plan BEFORE build (one sitting)
Write one page: the single channel that brings the first 100 visitors
(SEO, a marketplace, a community, a directory, my SO reputation), and why.
If I can't name the channel, the idea isn't validated — back to 3.
Exit: named channel + first 10 concrete actions on it.

### 5. Build the MVP (timeboxed, ≤4 weekends)
Smallest thing someone can pay for. Payment link on day one (Stripe/Lemon
Squeezy/Gumroad). Scope creep = the deadline wins, not the feature.
Exit: a stranger can pay me money without talking to me.

### 6. First dollar, then loop
Target: **first $1 from a stranger** — that's the real milestone; $100 MRR is
just repetition after it. Weekly: 1 distribution action minimum, measure,
improve or kill. Every kill goes back into stage 1 as learning.

## Operating rules
- Selling ≥ building: any week with zero distribution actions is a failed week,
  even if I shipped code.
- One experiment at a time past stage 3. Parallel only in validation.
- Kill deadlines are sacred. Sunk cost is the enemy of a person with a job.
- Progress metric until first revenue: **number of experiments completed**,
  not features shipped.
